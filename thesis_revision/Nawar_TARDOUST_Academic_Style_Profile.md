# Academic Style Profile

A concise profile of the thesis’s scholarly voice, with the conventions applied during
editing and recommendations for keeping any further writing consistent.

## Sentence-length profile
Moderate-to-long sentences (typically 20–35 words), interleaved with shorter declarative
sentences for verified decisions and definitions. Variation follows the logic of the
argument: direct statements for methodological choices, cautious modal constructions for
interpretation, contrastive structures where studies disagree.

## Preferred paragraph structure
Topic sentence stating an analytical claim → evidence, comparison, or explanation →
qualification or boundary → a linking sentence where the next idea requires one. Literature
paragraphs synthesise several studies around one point rather than listing them.

## Citation-integration patterns
Both narrative (“Ahuja and Katila (2001) find…”) and parenthetical (“(Jensen, 1986)”)
forms are used, with grouped synthesis for converging evidence (“(Ahuja & Katila, 2001;
Cloodt et al., 2006; Valentini, 2012)”) and contrastive attribution where results differ.
Citations sit adjacent to the claim they support.

## Hedging profile
Calibrated to evidence. Strong verbs (“the analysis identifies”, “the results show”) for
verified findings; moderate verbs (“the findings suggest”, “the evidence is consistent
with”) for supported interpretation; tentative forms (“one possible explanation is”, “may
reflect”, “cannot be established directly with the available data”) for conjecture.
Neither uniform certainty nor blanket hedging.

## Active/passive balance
Restrained passive for procedures where the actor is immaterial (“the sample was capped
at 2023”); active voice for the thesis’s own analytical choices (“the present study
compares…”). First-person “we/our” is avoided in favour of “the present study”, “this
thesis”, or “the analysis”.

## Vocabulary principles
Precise over inflated. Avoid: *multifaceted, intricate, dynamic landscape, pivotal,
transformative, groundbreaking, undeniable, profound, sheds light on*, and *robust* /
*comprehensive* unless literally assessed. Prefer the exact verb.

## Recurring terminology (use consistently)
innovation-driven acquisition · alternative-rationale acquisition · patent-family output ·
return on assets (ROA) · differential post-acquisition change · acquirer · completion year ·
Difference-in-Differences · event study · classification error · innovation–finance
divergence · acquirer-group (clustering unit) · estimand.

## Preferred British-English forms
favourable, organisation, commercialisation, modelling, normalise, generalisability,
analyse, labour, programme (not “program” for a plan/agenda), centre. Apply throughout;
the source mixed these with US spellings, which the revision standardised.

## Section-specific recommendations
- **Introduction / literature review:** synthesise and compare; avoid author-by-author
  chronology; lead each subsection to the hypotheses.
- **Methodology:** direct prose; state completed procedures precisely; define the estimand;
  avoid decorative language around technical steps; keep every number and variable exact.
- **Results:** report before interpreting; state coefficients, intervals, and p-values
  precisely; distinguish statistical from economic significance; avoid causal verbs.
- **Discussion:** interpret rather than restate; consider alternative explanations; mark
  the difference between plausible and tested mechanisms; hedge in proportion to evidence.
- **Conclusion:** answer the research question; no new literature or evidence; concise.

## Prohibited clichés (do not reintroduce)
“Since the dawn of time”, “In today’s rapidly changing world”, “It is widely known that”,
rhetorical questions, and chains of paragraph-initial “Furthermore / Moreover / However /
Additionally”.

## Cautions to preserve
Never equate statistical insignificance with equivalence; never describe a directionally
consistent estimate as confirmation; never call H2 “partially confirmed”; never use
“control group” to imply non-acquiring firms; keep patent *quantity* distinct from
innovation *quality*, and ROA distinct from total shareholder value.
